Fleet battery over the regatta

Findings

1 · Race-day battery burn ≈ 2× the bench model on the long-course day

  • 07-09 racing: fleet median 1.66 %/h ≈ 0.8× the calibrated 115 mA model.
  • 07-10 (long distance): fleet median 3.15 %/h ≈ 1.4×, tails to 5–5.8 %/h (≈2×), on both firmware families; fleet median resting drop 180 mV that day vs 30 mV on 07-09.
  • Ruled out: upload rate (1.08 LOC/s both days), GSM signal during racing (better on 07-10), overnight drain (~10 mV both nights).
  • Planning figure: budget ~2 full race days per 6 Ah charge on long courses; recharge after any long-distance day. Four units ended the regatta under 15 % SoC (G375430 1.6 %, G347082 2.0 %, G375562 12 %, G312268 13 %); the fleet median finished at 45 %.

2 · Idle GPS-restart flurries are an NT19D-V667 signature

  • 11 idle-LOC burst episodes during the regatta — every one on a V667 unit (0.08/10 idle-h); zero on W07 V6.6x under identical idle config.
  • Pattern: off-schedule no-fix LOC → server re-asserts GPS-off → GPS resets, briefly acquires (sats 10–13), uploads 4–6 fixes, settles. Self-limiting; none needed a MODE1 bounce.

3 · Blind buffer worked exactly as designed

  • Two of the four replay episodes were caused by spontaneous mid-race device reboots (G347082 & G341846, 07-10, both V667, ~2 min offline): the backlog replayed on reconnect (max lag 136 s / 114 s) and drained to live in 3m14s / 2m14s. No track data lost.
  • The other two: a 44 s cellular blip mid-race (G341887, day 1 — see case study) and one 34-hour-stale cached fix replayed into a live stream (G378723, the known stale-fix quirk).
  • V667 drains the buffer interleaved — live fixes slotted between backlog fixes. Messy on the wire, harmless after server timestamp handling; V6.68 drains in order.
  • Server lag remediation (TIMER,2 drain) never engaged and was never needed; no replay stalled.

4 · Reboots & server events

  • 18 device reboots (frame-serial regressions) during the regatta: 9 were bench power-cycles of the development unit (case study below), 4 followed operator RESET# commands, leaving 5 spontaneous: the two mid-race ones above plus three single idle units (G370589, G371116 — V667; G375372 — V6.68). Each cost at most a couple of minutes of contact.
  • 6 server-restart socket resets (device serials continue across all of them): 07-07 18:59, 07-08 05:41 & 18:13, 07-09 04:09, 06:09 & 16:43.
  • One tracker (G187547) was powered off before the window opened and never appeared during the regatta.
  • One −70 mV voltage step (G371116, 07-08 06:16, adjacent to its reboot) was the only sudden-drop event; one SOS alarm frame (G334189, 07-07 15:05).

Case studies

G341887 — the hardest-worked tracker (NT19D V667, 6 Ah)

Six race outings across all four days — 15.2 tracking hours, 54,185 fixes at a solid 1 Hz, GPS lag p50 1 s / p95 2 s, zero dropouts, all on a single charge: 4.07 V at the start of racing down to 3.64 V (≈25 %) at the finish. Its heaviest race (07-09 afternoon) burned 2.8 %/h ≈ 170 mA — the per-unit shape of the fleet-wide finding that real racing draws well above the 115 mA bench figure.

Ten minutes into its first race (07-07 10:23) it lost the network for 44 s, re-logged-in, and replayed 12 buffered fixes — fully live again in 71 s, in the V667 interleaved drain style. The sailor's track has no gap: the blind-buffer mechanism working end-to-end on day one.

G375356 — the custom-firmware unit (WSTRK V665, 3 Ah)

The one unit running our own firmware build spent the regatta ashore as the development bench unit, and its log reads like a bench diary: powered off 07-07 evening → 07-09 morning, then 9 reboots on 07-09 — every one a deliberate power-cycle during firmware-recovery development, and every one showing the clean serial-regression signature the reboot detector keys on (a useful validation of the method against known ground truth).

While connected and idle it held perfect cadence — heartbeat every 15 s, STATUS every 60 s, the expected one cached-fix upload per 30 min — and produced zero idle GPS-restart flurries in ~77 idle hours, unlike the stock V667 fleet (finding 2). A small sample, but consistent with the flurries being a V667-generation regression rather than something inherent to the hardware.

Firmware × capacity

Capacity class shows no anomaly correlation — 3 Ah and 6 Ah behave alike per hour and differ only in endurance. Firmware is the discriminator: all idle GPS flurries, both mid-race reboots and 4 of 5 spontaneous reboots are NT19D V667; the W07 V6.6x units were essentially silent.
