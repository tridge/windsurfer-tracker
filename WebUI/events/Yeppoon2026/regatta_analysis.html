Fleet battery through the week

The 07-07 01:35 cellular outage & mass device reboot

Contact fell from 40 to 16 units in minutes while the server kept serving the rest — a partial mobile-network outage, not a server or power event. Every affected unit returned at 01:50–02:01 with a frame-serial regression: the firmware watchdog reboots the whole device after ~15 minutes without network registration. Recovery was autonomous and identical across firmware families.

Findings

1 · Race-day battery burn ≈ 2× the bench model on the long-course day

  • 07-09 racing: fleet median 1.66 %/h ≈ 0.8× the calibrated 115 mA model.
  • 07-10 (long distance): fleet median 3.15 %/h ≈ 1.4×, tails to 5–5.8 %/h (≈2×), on both firmware families; fleet median resting drop 180 mV that day vs 30 mV on 07-09.
  • Ruled out: upload rate (1.08 LOC/s both days), GSM signal during racing (better on 07-10), overnight drain (~10 mV both nights).
  • Planning figure: budget ~2 full race days per 6 Ah charge on long courses; recharge after any long-distance day. Six units finished the week under 15 % SoC.

2 · Idle GPS-restart flurries are an NT19D-V667 signature

  • 49 idle-LOC burst episodes: 46 on V667 (0.142/10 idle-h), 3 on V6.68 (0.010/10 h), zero on V6.63/V665 — a 14× firmware gap under identical idle config.
  • Pattern: off-schedule no-fix LOC → server re-asserts GPS-off → GPS resets, briefly acquires (sats 10–13), uploads 4–6 fixes, settles. Self-limiting; only 2 episodes reached the ≥5 LOC/min “stuck” rate; none needed a MODE1 bounce.

3 · Blind buffer worked exactly as designed

  • Both mid-race reboots (G347082 & G341846, 07-10, V667, ~2 min offline) were fully covered: backlog replayed on reconnect (max lag 136 s / 114 s) and drained to live in 3m14s / 2m14s. No track data lost.
  • V667 drains the buffer interleaved — live fixes slotted between backlog fixes (135/74 GPS-time backward steps). Messy on the wire, harmless after server timestamp handling; V6.68 drains in order.
  • Server lag remediation (TIMER,2 drain) never engaged and was never needed; no replay stalled.

4 · Reboots & resets

  • 50 serial-regression reboots: ~24 in the 07-07 network event, 10 on the bench unit (G375356, USB surgery), 5 operator RESET#, 2 mid-race (above), several at 07-11 pack-up on nearly-flat cells.
  • ~6 genuinely spontaneous single-unit reboots remain (≈1 per 1000 unit-h) — five of six on V667.
  • 9 server-restart socket resets (device serials continue across all of them) — freeze-week timestamps worth reconciling with ops notes: 07-05 12:07/12:17/20:08, 07-07 18:59, 07-08 05:41/18:13, 07-09 04:09/06:09/16:43.

5 · Battery-health outliers

  • G312268 (3 Ah) is a suspect cell: 0.98 %/h idle drain on 07-11 (10× model), worst overnight drops both nights, −120 mV resting step — same fingerprint as the known-bad G375356 cell. Bench-test before the next event.
  • G380521: double −110 mV step + reboot on 07-05 morning (handling); clean afterwards. G371116: −70 mV near its 07-08 reboot.

Firmware × capacity

Capacity class shows no anomaly correlation (G312268's bad cell aside) — 3 Ah and 6 Ah behave alike per hour and differ only in endurance. Firmware is the discriminator: the idle GPS flurries, both mid-race reboots and nearly all spontaneous reboots are NT19D V667; the W07 V6.6x units were essentially silent.
