Fleet battery over the regatta

Findings

1 · Race-day battery burn ≈ 2× the bench model on the long-course day

  • 07-09 racing: fleet median 1.66 %/h ≈ 0.8× the calibrated 115 mA model.
  • 07-10 (long distance): fleet median 3.15 %/h ≈ 1.4×, tails to 5–5.8 %/h (≈2×), on both firmware families; fleet median resting drop 180 mV that day vs 30 mV on 07-09.
  • Ruled out: upload rate (1.08 LOC/s both days), GSM signal during racing (better on 07-10), overnight drain (~10 mV both nights).
  • Planning figure: budget ~2 full race days per 6 Ah charge on long courses; recharge after any long-distance day. Four units ended the regatta under 15 % SoC (G375430 1.6 % — see case study, G347082 2.0 %, G375562 12 %, G312268 13 %); the fleet median finished at 45 %.

2 · Idle GPS-restart flurries are an NT19D-V667 signature

  • 11 idle-LOC burst episodes during the regatta — every one on a V667 unit (0.08/10 idle-h); zero on W07 V6.6x under identical idle config.
  • Pattern: off-schedule no-fix LOC → server re-asserts GPS-off → GPS resets, briefly acquires, uploads a few fixes, settles. Self-limiting; none needed a MODE1 bounce. A frame-by-frame worked example is in the G341846 case study.

3 · Blind buffer worked exactly as designed

  • Two of the four replay episodes were caused by mid-race device reboots (G347082 & G341846, 07-10, both ~2 min offline): the backlog replayed on reconnect (max lag 136 s / 114 s) and drained to live in 3m14s / 2m14s. No track data lost. G347082's was a low-battery brown-out; G341846's, with a rock-steady cell, was a genuine firmware reboot — both are case studies below.
  • The other two: a 44 s cellular blip mid-race (G341887, day 1 — see case study) and one 34-hour-stale cached fix replayed into a live stream (G378723, the known stale-fix quirk).
  • V667 drains the buffer interleaved — live fixes slotted between backlog fixes. Messy on the wire, harmless after server timestamp handling; V6.68 drains in order.
  • Server lag remediation (TIMER,2 drain) never engaged and was never needed; no replay stalled.

4 · Reboots & server events

  • 18 device reboots (frame-serial regressions) during the regatta: 9 were bench power-cycles of the development unit (case study below), 4 followed operator RESET# commands, leaving 5 unexplained by operator action: one mid-race brown-out on a collapsing battery (G347082, case study), one genuinely spontaneous mid-race reboot (G341846, V667 — case study) and three single idle units (G370589, G371116 — V667; G375372 — V6.68). Each cost at most a couple of minutes of contact.
  • 6 server-restart socket resets (device serials continue across all of them): 07-07 18:59, 07-08 05:41 & 18:13, 07-09 04:09, 06:09 & 16:43.
  • One tracker (G187547) was powered off before the window opened and never appeared during the regatta.
  • One −70 mV voltage step (G371116, 07-08 06:16, adjacent to its reboot) was the only sudden-drop event; one SOS alarm frame (G334189, 07-07 15:05).

Case studies

G341887 — the hardest-worked tracker (NT19D V667, 6 Ah)

Six race outings across all four days — 15.2 tracking hours, 54,185 fixes at a solid 1 Hz, GPS lag p50 1 s / p95 2 s, zero dropouts, all on a single charge: 4.07 V at the start of racing down to 3.64 V (≈25 %) at the finish. Its heaviest race (07-09 afternoon) burned 2.8 %/h ≈ 170 mA — the per-unit shape of the fleet-wide finding that real racing draws well above the 115 mA bench figure.

It is also the clean control for finding 2: not a single idle GPS-restart flurry all regatta — 130 idle uploads, every one on the 30-minute cadence, and GPS reported OFF in every idle STATUS poll.

Ten minutes into its first race (07-07 10:23) it lost the network for 44 s, re-logged-in, and replayed 12 buffered fixes — fully live again in 71 s, in the V667 interleaved drain style. The sailor's track has no gap: the blind-buffer mechanism working end-to-end on day one.

G375430 — the battery that looked fine until it wasn't (W07 V6.68, 6 Ah)

For three days this unit was the picture of health: GPS off in all 3,903 idle STATUS replies, a textbook ~25 mV/day resting decline (4.13 → 4.05 V), the expected one cached-fix upload per 30 min. By the standard voltage→charge curve it started the final day at roughly 75 %.

Then it raced — its only racing of the regatta, 2.6 hours on the 07-10 long-distance day — and the voltage collapsed from 4.05 V to 3.30 V within the racing morning, finishing the regatta effectively flat (1.6 %, the lowest in the fleet). A healthy 6 Ah cell burns a few percent in 2.6 h of tracking; this one gave up ~75 points. That pattern — normal resting voltage, capacity collapse under sustained load — is a weak-cell signature, not a firmware or configuration problem. Notably its calibration capacity was an estimate (it never completed the full discharge test that firmed up most of the fleet), so nothing in the data before 07-10 could have exposed it.

A separate quirk: its GPS fixes arrive consistently ~7 s stale (lag p50 7 s / p95 8 s — the fleet's worst, vs 3 s / 4 s typical), a steady device-side delay rather than backlog. Flagged for a bench discharge test before the next event.

Re-test after a full recharge (07-12). Two days after the regatta the unit was recharged and taken on a ~1¾-hour tracking run at real sailing speeds (up to 20 kn, solid 1 Hz, 6,193 fixes). From a fresh charge the collapse did not reproduce: 4.07 V resting at the start, 4.02 V under load at the end — no measurable state-of-charge loss at the 10 mV resolution, with a single −70 mV transient sag (10:29) that recovered within minutes. Its GPS staleness was also normal on this run (fix lag p50/p95 = 2 s, against ~7 s throughout the regatta).

That narrows the diagnosis: the cell still performs on the upper part of its charge, and the regatta failure mode was state-of-charge dependent — after three idle days had eaten into a partial charge, the remaining real capacity was far less than the voltage curve implied, and race load fell off the cliff. The unit can be trusted for a freshly-charged single session, not for multi-day events without daily charging; a bench discharge to measure its true capacity is the follow-up.

G347082 — G375430's twin, plus a brown-out mid-race (NT19D V667, 6 Ah)

Same script as G375430, on a different unit and firmware family: three days of perfectly healthy idle (91 % → 85 % indicated, GPS off throughout), the same two 07-10 race windows as its twin — its only racing of the regatta — and then 4.04 V → 3.37 V inside the first 56-minute race. It finished the regatta at 3.29 V ≈ 2 %.

The collapse explains its "spontaneous" reboot: at 13:21, mid-way through the second race with the cell already at ~3.3 V, it dropped off for ~2 minutes and came back with a serial regression — almost certainly a low-battery brown-out, not a firmware fault. The blind buffer earned its keep: on reconnect it replayed 135 buffered fixes (max 136 s stale) and was fully live in 3m14s, so the sailor's track is continuous through the reboot.

With G312268 below, three units across both firmware families showed the identical signature on the same day: normal resting voltage all week, then capacity collapse within hours of sustained tracking load. Both had been charged in the same waves on the morning of 07-07 and both raced only the long-distance day. This unit's parked-idle behaviour was separately verified as normal (~6 mA) in a June diagnosis — the cell holds its voltage but no longer holds its charge. Both are flagged for bench discharge tests before the next event.

G312268 — the third collapse, with a tell (W07 V6.68, 3 Ah)

The third member of the collapse pattern, from the 3 Ah class: three days of normal idle (4.19 → 4.09 V resting, ~94 % → 91 % indicated), the same two 07-10 races as the others — and it finished the regatta at 13 %.

This one carries a diagnostic tell the twins don't: the moment race load was applied its voltage sagged from 4.09 V resting to 3.54 V — roughly 550 mV, where a healthy cell sags 40–80 mV under the same ~115 mA. That is an internal-resistance signature about 10× normal, visible in the very first minute of tracking. During the races the reading barely moved (3.54 → 3.46 V, because most of it was IR drop), while the real charge drained away underneath: its measured race drain was 5.0 %/h against 3.3 %/h expected, and an hour after racing it had settled at 3.46 V ≈ 13 % — real depletion, not sag. It also re-logged-in twice within seconds at the second race's end, consistent with the cell dipping at load transients.

The practical lesson: unlike the twins, this cell was catchable before the start — a loaded-vs-resting voltage comparison in the first minutes of tracking (a "sag screen") would have flagged it while swapping units was still possible. Worth automating for the next event.

G341846 — the healthy control, and the one true mid-race reboot (NT19D V667, 6 Ah)

The fourth unit that raced only the two 07-10 long-distance races — same workload as the three collapsed units above, and the reason we can be sure their story is the battery, not the day: G341846 sailed the same courses (3.1 tracking hours, 11,669 fixes, lag p50 4 s / p95 5 s) and its battery barely noticed — 3.89 → 3.83 V across both races, finishing the regatta near 60 %.

What makes it a case study is what happened at 13:53, at speed in the middle of the second race: the unit dropped off for 123 s and came back with a serial regression — a reboot. Unlike its twin G347082 forty minutes earlier, this was not a brown-out: the voltage was rock-steady at 3.85 V in the minutes before and after (no sag whatsoever). This is the one genuine spontaneous V667 firmware reboot under way in the whole regatta, and the concrete case for the vendor list. The cost to the sailor: nothing — the blind buffer replayed 74 fixes (max 114 s stale) on reconnect and was fully live in 2m14s, so the track is continuous through the reboot.

It also supplies the cleanest frame-by-frame example of a V667 idle GPS-restart flurry (finding 2), on the afternoon of day 1 — and it wasn't alone: G370498 and G334189, which had entered idle at the same moment after the morning session and so share the same 30-minute wake phase, ran the identical sequence in the same twelve minutes. The wake → no-fix → reset → instant 9-satellite lock pattern shows the GPS receiver was wedged while the sky view was fine — exactly the condition the idle GPS_RST_TIME=60 setting exists to recover:

G375356 — the custom-firmware unit (WSTRK V665, 3 Ah)

The one unit running our own firmware build spent the regatta ashore as the development bench unit, and its log reads like a bench diary: powered off 07-07 evening → 07-09 morning, then 9 reboots on 07-09 — every one a deliberate power-cycle during firmware-recovery development, and every one showing the clean serial-regression signature the reboot detector keys on (a useful validation of the method against known ground truth).

While connected and idle it held perfect cadence — heartbeat every 15 s, STATUS every 60 s, the expected one cached-fix upload per 30 min — and produced zero idle GPS-restart flurries in ~77 idle hours, unlike the stock V667 fleet (finding 2). A small sample, but consistent with the flurries being a V667-generation regression rather than something inherent to the hardware.

Firmware × capacity

Capacity class shows no anomaly correlation — 3 Ah and 6 Ah behave alike per hour and differ only in endurance. Firmware is the discriminator: all idle GPS flurries and 3 of 4 spontaneous reboots are NT19D V667; the W07 V6.6x units were essentially silent.

  • Spontaneous reboots (3 of 4 on V667, one of them mid-race).
